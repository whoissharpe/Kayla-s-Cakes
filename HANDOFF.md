# Your new website — how it works

Hi Kayla! Here's the plain-language version of what you've got and how to use
it. No technical knowledge needed for any of this.

## What the site does

People land on your homepage, see your cakes, and fill in a form telling you
what they want — the date, the flavor, the size, the theme, and photos of
whatever inspired them. It asks for exactly the same things your Instagram
post asks for, so nothing gets missed.

The moment somebody sends that form, the order shows up on your private
orders page.

**Email alerts aren't switched on yet.** They need a domain name of your own
(like kaylascakes.com) before they can work, so for now the orders page is the
only place new orders appear. Keep it open in a pinned tab — the number of new
orders shows right in the tab, so you can see at a glance without clicking.
Once you pick a domain we'll turn the emails on.

## Your orders page

Go to **yoursite.com/admin** and sign in with your password.

Everything anyone has ever sent you is there, newest first. For each one you
can see all their details and any inspiration photos they attached, and:

- **Change the status.** New → Quoted → Booked → Completed. Or Lost, if it
  doesn't work out. Just pick from the dropdown; it saves by itself.
- **Add your own notes.** There's a box on every order — "quoted $180,"
  "waiting on her to pick colors," whatever helps you remember. It saves when
  you click away.
- **Search.** Type a name, a flavor, anything.
- **Filter.** Tap "new" to see only the ones you haven't dealt with yet.
- **Export.** The "Export CSV" button downloads everything as a spreadsheet.

Orders happening in the next week get an orange tag so they're hard to miss.
Ones whose date has passed get a red one.

**Keep your password to yourself.** Anyone who has it can see every customer's
contact details.

Before this goes live, we need you to confirm a few things. Some of these are
currently guesses pulled off the internet and could well be wrong:

1. **Your phone number.** We have `(904) 309-2270` — is that right, and is it
   the one you want customers texting?
2. **Your email address.** We have `kaylascakesbakedwithlove@gmail.com`.
3. **How far you'll travel.** Do you deliver at all, or is it pickup only? If
   you deliver, how far?
4. **Your prices.** Even rough ranges ("cakes usually start around $X") help
   people know if they're in the right place, and cut down on wasted messages.
5. **How much notice you need.** Two weeks? A month for big ones?
6. **Do you want to take payment online?** Right now the site collects the
   enquiry and you invoice separately, exactly like you do today. We can add
   online payment later if you'd like.
7. **The legal wording in the footer.** We've put the standard Florida cottage
   food sentence at the bottom of every page. Please check it says what you
   want it to say — and remember it doesn't replace the label on your boxes.
8. **Your bio.** The "Hi, I'm Kayla" section is a first draft we wrote to
   sound like you. Please rewrite it so it's actually true.

## Photos

The site is ready for your photos — we just need them from you. Send a folder
of your best work and we'll get them in. Right now there are grey placeholder
squares marked "Placeholder" so it's obvious what's missing.

The more photos the better, especially of different styles — kids' birthdays,
elegant ones, cupcakes, strawberries. That's what convinces someone you can do
the thing they're imagining.

## How to ask for changes

Just message with what you want changed and roughly where it is — "the bit
about pickup should say X," or "swap the third photo." Screenshots with
scribbles on them are genuinely the easiest thing to work from.

Small text changes are quick. Ask any time.
